# DREAM PROTOCOL

First-person psychological horror about lucid dreaming, unstable reality and subconscious fear.
Made with **Godot 4.7** (free, open source, no account needed). Runs on Mac, Windows and Linux.

Playable now: ten realms, each a full expedition with a beginning, middle, climax and escape:
bedroom → the summer **CITY** → the **BACKROOMS** → **THE DEEP** → **COMPRESSION** → **ABOVE** →
**BLACKOUT** → **STILL LIFE** → **OPEN SPACE** → **PATTERN** → **THE QUIET**. (The ending, THE DESTINATION, is next.)
Every area has an **objectives** list in the top-right corner.

---

## How to play it (Mac)

1. Download **Godot 4.7** (the normal version, not ".NET"): https://godotengine.org/download/macos/
   Unzip it and drag **Godot.app** into *Applications*.
2. Download this project: on GitHub click **Code → Download ZIP** (branch `claude/dream-protocol-dev-eom8wy`), and unzip it.
3. Open Godot. The first time macOS may say it can't verify the app: right-click Godot.app → **Open** → **Open**.
4. In the Project Manager click **Import**, choose the `project.godot` file inside the unzipped folder, then **Import & Edit**.
   The first import takes about a minute.
5. Press **▶ (Run Project)** at the top right, or **⌘B**.

That's it. No other downloads, no Xcode.

**If it runs slowly** (older or Intel Macs): Pause → MENU → SETTINGS → *Graphics quality* → **LOW**.
It takes effect when you enter the next area.

## What's in the slice

- **Bedroom, 3 AM.** Watch *"HOW TO LUCID DREAM TONIGHT! (100% WORKING)"* on the PC; the five rules
  get written into your notebook. Take the notebook. Go to bed.
- **The summer city** (explore, experiment). You wake in a sunlit copy of your bedroom: do a reality check
  (read the clock, look away, read it again) and go outside. Explore the neighbourhood (fountain, ice-cream
  cart, bus stop). Hold Q to picture things; change the dream (start the fountain, repaint a car, light the lamps).
  Talk to the residents - tell one of them they're dreaming and see what happens. Find the man who doesn't
  move (the **Observer**), then the quiet place to sleep: the hammock under the big tree on the hill.
- **The Backrooms** (navigate, investigate). Yellow rooms that rearrange themselves when unobserved; things
  from the city in them (a bench, a street sign, a melted ice-cream cart). A maintenance door leads to locked
  doors, a dark room behind glass with something very tall standing in it, and a power room. Three notes give
  the breaker order. Power on: the lights go red, the walls shift, two doorways lead elsewhere - and the glass
  breaks. Run back to the lift where you started. It opens onto water.
- **THE DEEP** (activate, discover, escape).
  1. A night-time swimming complex: the jammed PUMP ROOM (swim under), the snapped DRAIN lever (dive for the
     handle), water that only drains **while you watch it**, locker 13 of twelve. Step into the empty well.
  2. An impossibly vast sea. Black hands from the sea floor pull you under.
  3. **The seabed**: pitch dark, slow. Picture a flashlight. Glowing stones mark an invisible path - stray and
     the floor takes you. Something huge passes overhead. Through the glowing doorway in the sea cave:
  4. **The flooded maze**: narrow service tunnels; turn two valve wheels to open the bulkhead.
  5. **The rooms**: three doors (two open back into the same room); a room with no doors (walk through the wall
     the bubbles leak through); a door that only exists while you aren't looking at it.
  6. **The Abyssal Cross**: four trenches, a beacon across each. Light all four and a bridge of light reaches
     the station whose lights you saw from the start.
  7. **The leviathan**: the station goes dark. Out the far side, it comes for you across open sand. Run for the
     glowing door in the cliff.
- **COMPRESSION** (spatial puzzles). A basement: the way in becomes wall; tight spaces raise DREAD. Three fuses
  (the vent, **the press** - walls that move while unwatched - and **the drying room**) power a freight elevator
  that goes *up*, into a tenement that doesn't fit inside itself:
  apartment 3B is far bigger inside than the building (a park bench in the living room, a yellow humming hallway,
  a child's room, the Observer in its corner); the bathroom vent narrows as you crawl; in the stairwell going **up**
  brings you back to the same floor and going **down** raises the floor number - the roof is from floor 7;
  floor 7's corridor collapses a step behind you, ceiling and walls folding shut; a narrow shaft climbs to a door at the top.
- **ABOVE** (traverse, climb). Rooftops above the clouds at night. The red light of the mast on the last tower is
  visible from the very first roof.
  1. The plank over the drop for the key. 2. The steel beam (**crouch in the wind**; it won't be there behind you)
  and the bridge. 3. A sky lift that runs sideways through floors that don't exist. 4. Stairs in the open air,
  there only when you're close to them. 5. An unfinished tower: scaffolding, and a crane to swing a girder across
  the gap. 6. A glass skyway; the floor cracks and the lights die behind you. 7. Climb the mast and touch the
  light: the platform takes itself apart and builds a path into the sky, to a door.
  Looking down fills DREAD. Sometimes, far away, someone falls.
- **BLACKOUT** (the dark). Larkspur Lane after midnight, every house dark. Streetlights go out when you stop
  looking at them; stay in the dark too long and it takes you. A tree down across the road: through number 9,
  where a red darkroom bulb shows a key that isn't there under any other light. Round the back gardens to the
  junction box: every light comes on, and the road is full of people's shadows with nobody casting them. Then
  the dark comes back up the street, a lamp at a time. Run for the night bus.
- **STILL LIFE** (mannequins). The Emporium, after closing, the music still playing. The mannequins never move
  while you watch them. The escalator keys are on the security guard, who is a mannequin in a cap. On Level 2,
  THE PERFECT FAMILY at dinner, with a place laid for you; sit, and the way up opens, and they're all looking at
  you. Level 3, LIFELIKE: figures more real one after another, then people you know, then an empty plinth with
  your name on it. The lights fail behind you; in the dark they don't need you to look away.
- **OPEN SPACE** (wide open places). The staff exit opens onto a car park with no end under a vast pale sky; out in
  the open, fear builds by itself. Your car chirps somewhere and moves when unseen. The gas station on the horizon
  gets no closer while you look at it: watch the road. A payphone rings: it's Mina. Out the back of the shop, a
  plaza the size of a town, and subway stairs that move whenever you take your eyes off them.
- **PATTERN** (clusters of holes). A station tiled in hexagons; then the hive: a six-sided room with six doorways,
  every frame crusted with holes. Only the clean doorway goes on, and it moves when you look away; every other
  doorway brings you back into the same room as the holes spread. Four rooms deep, a vault of three rings, and
  behind it a hospital door: WARD 6.
- **THE QUIET** (death). A hospital at night. Take a number (13; they're serving 12). The clocks disagree. The
  visitors' book is in your handwriting: every night, 3:12 AM, Ward 6. Records for people who never existed
  (you know them all). Pool water in the corridor, visitors under sheets in the doorways. Ward 6 is your room,
  with a hospital bed in it; the chart says *not yet*. In the memorial garden Mr. Halvorsen gives you the key to
  the gate at the end. (The gate leads to the ending, not built yet.)
  Swimming: Space up, C dive, watch your breath. Space at the surface next to the edge climbs out.
- **Every entity kills differently**: the tall one in the Backrooms, the leviathan's jaws, the sheet figures,
  the corridor folding shut, the long fall, the hands from the sea floor, the dark, the mannequins, the holes,
  the Observer when DREAD overwhelms you.
  Every death wakes you in your bedroom. Probably.
- **The notebook** gains new lines at milestones - in your handwriting, and not always agreeing with the old ones.
- **Dream journal** (title screen, or Pause > MENU): an entry for every part of the dream you have made it through.
- **The yellow maze.** You fall through the floor instead. Fluorescent hum, stained carpet, a maze that
  is always solvable but rearranges itself when you aren't looking. Your notebook has a sixth rule now.
- **The Observer** glitches - slices of him shear and drop out. Spotting him raises DREAD by 25, and he
  glitches out of existence a second or two later.
- **DREAD** (fear + dream stability) drives vignette, desaturation, grain, flickering lights, a low drone,
  knocks behind you, how often the maze changes and when the Observer appears.
- **The pause menu** may not be entirely a pause menu at very high DREAD.
- **If DREAD reaches 100** you wake up in your bedroom. Probably.
- **Checkpoints** at the start of each area; **Continue** on the title screen.

## Languages

English and **한국어 (Korean)**: Settings > Language / 언어. On a Korean system or browser the game starts in Korean.
Translations live in `data/Lang_ko.json` (English text -> Korean, plus patterns for text built at runtime);
`scripts/core/loc_translation.gd` applies them to every label. In-world signs stay in English.
Korean fonts: `fonts/` (renamed subsets of NAVER's Nanum fonts, SIL Open Font License).
`tests/loc_check.tscn` spot-checks runtime-built text; `flow_test.tscn -- --lang=ko --loc-misses` lists anything untranslated.

## Accessibility (Settings)

Mouse sensitivity, invert look, field of view, head bob, camera jolts, **visual intensity** (flicker,
vignette, distortion, red flash), **horror audio intensity**, master volume, text size, DREAD meter,
menu corruption on/off, self-unpause on/off, show objectives, graphics quality, and **super duper shaders** (global illumination,
reflections, volumetric fog, anti-aliasing - needs a strong GPU).

## Project layout

```
project.godot            Godot project (open this)
scenes/main.tscn         entry point - everything else is built in code (no binary assets)
models/                  OPTIONAL real art: drop a .glb here to replace a built-in person, the Observer,
                         an object or a surface texture (see models/README.md - no Blender needed)
data/                    dialogue, notebook rules, manifestable objects, tutorial script (JSON)
scripts/autoload/        Game (story flow, zones, checkpoints, sequences, DREAD director)
                         Settings, Data, Sfx (procedural audio), Notebook, Dialogue, Anomalies
scripts/core/            Observation ("is the player looking at this?"), Effects, Shapes, Interactable,
                         Models (finds, sizes and animates optional model files)
scripts/player/          Player controller (incl. swimming/breath), FPHands, Dread, Manifestation, ManifestedObject
scripts/world/           Zone (base + geometry builder), BedroomZone, CityZone, MazeZone, DeepZone, SeabedZone,
                         AbyssZone, CompressionZone, TenementZone, AboveZone, BlackoutZone, StillLifeZone,
                         OpenSpaceZone, PatternZone, QuietZone; water, caustics and sky shaders
data/Objectives.json     the objective list for every area
scripts/npc/             Human (procedural people), ModelBody (people from model files), NPC,
                         Observer (+ glitch shader), Stalker (the Backrooms' entity), Mannequin (STILL LIFE)
scripts/ui/              HUD, menus, notebook, tutorial video, DREAD post-process shader
tests/flow_test.tscn     automated playthrough of everything, title to the garden gate in THE QUIET, plus every death
tests/realms_test.tscn   the realms after ABOVE on their own (-- blackout / stilllife / openspace / pattern / quiet)
tests/qa_test.tscn       walks the risky routes with real input (stairs, chases, doors, the mannequins)
tests/gallery.tscn       renders review screenshots of each area
tools/creatures/          Blender (bpy) generators for the custom creature models -> models/*.glb (not used by the game)
unreal/                  the earlier Unreal Engine 5 version (archived, ignored by Godot)
```

**Key ideas**
- **Zones.** Each realm is a `Zone` subclass with its own geometry, light, audio, characters, anomalies
  and dream rules (fear gain, fear floor, anomaly pacing, whether manifestation works). Adding THE DEEP
  later means adding one new zone script.
- **Unobserved change** is a shared system: zones register anomalies ("this wall may toggle", "this light
  may die"); `Anomalies` decides when, based on dream stability, and only while nobody is looking.
- **Data-driven story.** Dialogue, rules, objects and the video script are JSON. Effects are strings like
  `fear:+10`, `rule:break:rule_tell`, `notebook:rewrite:rule_scary:spiral`, `observer:closer`.
  New Game reloads the JSON, so you can edit the story and test immediately.
- **No assets.** Geometry is built from primitives with procedural surface textures (tiles, wallpaper, wood,
  carpet, concrete, fabric), people are articulated procedural bodies with faces, the first-person hands are
  procedural too, all audio and music is synthesised at startup, all UI is code.
  Everything can be read, diffed and reviewed as text.
- **Real art is optional.** Put a model in `models/` (e.g. `models/people/mina.glb`, `models/observer.glb`,
  `models/textures/wood/`) and it replaces the built-in version, sized and animated automatically.
  Remove it and the built-in version is back. See **models/README.md**.
